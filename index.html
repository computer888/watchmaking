Introduction

Before you start

The last picture is a stereogram. Simply cross your eyes to put the two shapes together (research how to view stereograms). It shows a 3D-like picture in your mind of how the watch is made.

This book explains watchmaking basics with equations and pictures. Learn the physics, not just for the poor man: watches are highly precise mechanisms, and you should know the equation below first by using your brain to calculate the poor man’s watch.

If you can’t follow the equation below, I’m sorry, then you have no basis for making such a sophisticated mechanism from scratch. It’s an easy equation for equators, and watches are an intense piece of engineering.

The founding equation

First off, these physics make a poor watch

All watches are made from the one equation below. Other books won’t explain this in its entirety. It is explained briefly because not everyone knows physics and high-end mathematical formulae.

We have “T” = Time, multiplied by “T” time, divided by ^2 (squared), plus ^2 (squared), equals “T” time multiplied by “T” time.

TT ÷ square + square = TT“TT divided by square plus square equals TT”

The whole movement, the mechanisms needed and how it’s put together for all watches is the above mathematical formula. Nice work!

Now if you can draw out a watch blueprint with the above equation, you have a basic watch, a quartz movement as well, also automatic and windup. That is the founding equation used 100’s of years in the making of watchmaking.

Method 1

Laser cutting parts, plastic watch parts?

(A little more physics: “Tether in advancement of the parts”.) Every part connects like a puzzle. A laser cutter for acrylic or thin metals is your easiest route to make watch parts. You can blueprint your own or use a blueprint already made. The calculation you use will show you need calculations to successfully make watch parts with a laser cutter.

  • You need a good laser cutter with about 0.06 mm precision.
  • If you want a high-quality watch, go buy one. If you want a piece of crap just to tell the time, read this book. Lol.
  • This is only about how the parts are made. You have to do your homework on the blueprints and assembly.
  • You need a steady hand for the small parts and magnifying eyewear to see them.
  • A very basic tool set for precision parts. You are making every part that is in or on the watch.
  • You can buy cases, so you don’t need to cast metal, and you don’t need to cut glass. Use a case already made, plus a leather watch strap found just about anywhere, even in retail stores.
Method 2

Putting it together

Take the parts and assemble the factors by groups in consecutives of 50’s (physics again, because I cannot explain what I’m trying to teach with just words, as mathematics backs my word entirely). This is so you’re not wasting your time making a part that doesn’t fit.

You can buy a watch for 5 or 10 bucks. Why make your own with 1,000’s of dollars? Because if you make one, you can make more and start a watch business all on your own. The whole watch business is in that one equation above.

Method 3

How to make miscellaneous parts

Wood dials, melted metals for bands and cases, optical crystal for glasses. You need to know how to melt metals into molds, and have the right grinders, sanders, mills, lathes, presses and dies, plus machinery to make machinery. You also want a nice optical crystal glass cutting machine, and other tools for metal jewelry.

Misc parts are not always needed, but it’s great to have them available to your liking.

Parts machining calculator

Speeds, feeds and fit

For mills and lathes. Pick a material, enter your tool and cut, and get spindle speed and feed rate.

–Spindle speed (RPM)
–Feed rate (mm/min)
–Material removal (mm³/min)
–Cut time per part (min)
–Batch cut time (min)

RPM = cutting speed × 1000 ÷ (π × diameter). Feed = RPM × flutes × feed per tooth. Cutting speeds are rough starting values; check your tool maker’s data and start gently on tiny parts.

Tolerance check

–Smallest part (mm)
–Largest part (mm)
–Clearance to allow (mm)

Clearance is the worst case when a shaft and a hole each vary by ±precision (2 × 2 × tolerance).
